# M5 walkthrough: Written music (S22 Ultra, Android 16)

Builds: 0.5.0–0.5.2. Nothing here has run on the phone yet.

## 1. Transcription → tab (0.5.0)
Use a bass riff you recorded (plan §14.3: bass in D standard, ~92 BPM, with the click if you have one) that already has its MIDI (AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI) and a BPM.
1. On its MIDI tab, **MAKE TAB ▾** › pick the tuning you played it in (e.g. "Bass D standard"). A tab item opens.
2. Read the tab against what you played: the strings and frets, the bars (does bar 1 start on your first downbeat?), the rhythm under it (stems, beams within each beat, dots, ties over the barline, rests).
3. Tap a fret number: SELECTED names the pitch, string and fret, its length, and where else it can be played. Check the degree against the key.
4. On the fretboard, tap a hollow dot: the note moves to that string. **EDIT ▾ › Undo** puts it back.
5. Tap any other fret on the fretboard: you hear that note.
6. **ROW ▾ › Scale degrees** (the recording needs a key): the row under the tab shows degrees instead of names.
7. **▶ PLAY**: the tab plays on a soft tone and the cursor follows it.
8. Back on the Board or in the Library, the tab's window shows its first two bars.
9. Try it on a guitar take in standard tuning with chords: each chord should sit on separate strings within a hand span.

## 2. Editing tab (0.5.1)
1. **WRITE ▾ › Bass tab**: four empty bars at the metronome's tempo. **TUNING ▾ › Bass D standard › Keep the notes**.
2. Enter your riff: pick 1/8 on the duration bar, tap a fret on the fretboard, **NEXT ›**, and on. A fret on a second string makes a chord; REST clears a beat; TIE ties it on.
3. Make a bar too long or short on purpose: its number turns red and SELECTED says by how much.
4. **EFFECTS ▾ › Dynamics · tone › Palm mute** on a note: "P.M." above it. Try a hammer-on and a slide in **Touch**.
5. **EDIT ▾**: copy bar 1, paste it after bar 2; transpose up a semitone and back down; delete a beat; undo all of it.
6. **TUNING ▾ › Bass E standard › Keep the fingering**: the frets stay and every pitch moves up a tone.
7. ▶ PLAY: does it sound like what you meant?

## 3. Notation (0.5.2)
1. Open the tab from section 1, view ▾ › **Notation**. Check against the tab: bass clef, written an octave above what sounds (the low E under the staff), the key signature for the recording's key, accidentals only where the bar needs them, stems (middle line and above down), beams within each beat, dots and ties.
2. **WRITE ▾ › Sheet music**. INPUT ▾ › **Piano keys**: pick 1/8, tap keys with NEXT › between; turn **+ CHORD** on and tap three keys for a chord.
3. INPUT ▾ › **Tap on the staff**: tap lines and spaces; in a key with sharps or flats, the signature's notes come out altered.
4. Select a note, **ADD ▾ › Accidentals › ♯ Sharp**, then **Respell** a few times: the note moves between its spellings (C♯, D♭, B𝄪) without changing sound.
5. Play it with ▶ PLAY and watch the cursor.
6. Look hard at anything that reads wrong (a stem, a beam, a sharp in the wrong place): tell me which bar.

## 4. Markings (0.5.3)
Use a sheet-music item (WRITE ▾ › Sheet music) with a few bars of notes in it; select a beat or a note first, then ADD ▾.
1. **Articulations**: accent, staccato and tenuto on one note, a fermata on another. Marks sit on the notehead side (below when the stem is up), the fermata above.
2. **Dynamics · hairpins**: *mf* on beat 1, then **Crescendo hairpin › over 4 beats**: the wedge should open to the right under the staff. Try one that runs past the end of a line of music: it should carry on at the start of the next.
3. **Lines › Slur** over 3 beats, then **8va** over 4: the notes under it drop an octave on the page, the bracket says 8va. Tap the staff under the 8va: the note you get should be the octave you see plus one.
4. **Repeats · barlines**: Start repeat on bar 1, End repeat on bar 2, **1st ending** on bar 2 and **2nd ending** on bar 3. ▶ PLAY: bars 1 2 1 3, and the cursor goes round with it. Try **D.C. al Fine** with a **Fine** earlier.
5. **Clefs · keys · meters**: a key change and a 3/4 bar halfway through: the new signature and meter appear at that bar; the bars after it turn red where the notes no longer fill them.
6. **Text · tempo · rehearsal**: rehearsal mark A on bar 1, B later; a tempo change to 60 on bar 3 (playback slows there); *dolce* as expression; pizz. as technique.
7. **Lyrics › Verse 1**: type a syllable, HYPHEN –, the next, NEXT ›, … The words go under each sung note, skipping rests and tied notes. Add a verse 2 under the first.
8. **Chord symbols**: type Am7, F#m7b5, Bb13(#11), C/E: they show with ♯ and ♭. On a guitar tab in notation view, **Diagram from these frets** on a chord.
9. **Fingering**, **Noteheads** (try x and diamond), **Tremolo** (2 strokes on a half note), **Grace notes** (an acciaccatura, then step it down a few times).
10. EDIT ▾ › Transpose up a semitone: chord symbols move with the notes.
11. Close and reopen the item: everything is still there.
12. Tell me which bar anything looks wrong in: a mark on the wrong side, text over notes, a line that ends in the wrong place.

## 5. Input modes and voices (0.5.4)
Use a new sheet music item (WRITE ▾ › Sheet music), piano keys.
1. **MODE ▾ › Rhythm**: tap 1/4, 1/8, 1/8, 1/2: four notes appear on the middle line with those lengths. Set DOT first, then 1/4, for a dotted quarter.
2. **MODE ▾ › Re-pitch**: select the first note and play C, E, G, C on the piano keys: the notes take those pitches and keep their rhythm. Tie two notes first and check the tied one follows.
3. **MODE ▾ › Insert**: select beat 2 and play a note: it goes in before beat 2, the bar's number goes red; play another and it goes in after the first, still before the old beat 2.
4. **MODE ▾ › Real-time**: pick 1/8, START CLICK, hold a key for four clicks, then two keys together for two clicks, let go. STOP CLICK: you should see a half note and a chord, not a row of tied eighths. Check the metronome tool's tempo afterwards is what it was.
5. **MODE ▾ › Real-time, tapped**: hold a key and tap ADVANCE at your own pace; tap ADVANCE with nothing held for a rest; DONE.
6. **VOICE ▾ › Enter in voice 2**: back in Step mode, enter a bass line under a melody: voice 1 stems go up, voice 2 down, the rests move apart, and V2 shows green.
7. Select a chord in voice 1, **VOICE ▾ › Move C4 to voice 2** where voice 2 has a beat of the same length at that time; try where it doesn't: it says why.
8. **Hide this rest** on a voice 2 rest: it goes faint. **Clear voice 2 in this bar**.
9. ▶ PLAY: both voices sound. Switch to the tab view on a guitar part with two voices: voice 2's frets show in green.
10. Tell me about anything that collides or reads wrong, with the bar number.

## 6. Piano roll (0.5.5)
1. Open a sheet music item with a few bars, view ▾ › **Piano roll**. The notes are bars of colour at their pitches; tied notes are one bar.
2. TOOL Draw, SNAP 1/16: tap an empty spot: a sixteenth appears and sounds. Switch view ▾ › Notation: it's there, with rests round it.
3. Back in the roll, drag a note right and up: it moves by the snap and by semitones. Drag a note's right end: it lengthens; across a barline it becomes a tie in notation.
4. **SCALE** on (the item needs a key): drawn notes land on the key's scale.
5. TOOL Select, tap three notes, **TOOLS ▾ › Legato**, then **Quantize** after nudging one off the grid with SNAP 1/32.
6. **EDIT ▾ › Duplicate**: the selection repeats after itself.
7. Put a triplet in bar 1 (step mode), then edit only bar 3 in the roll: bar 1's triplet must be untouched.
8. A note with a fermata and a lyric: drag it later; the fermata and the syllable go with it.
9. Scroll by dragging empty space; ZOOM − / +.

## 7. Chord chart (0.5.6)
1. Open a sheet music item with four bars, view ▾ › **Chord chart**: two bars a row, a slash per beat.
2. Tap bar 1 beat 1, type Dm7, Done: it moves to beat 2. Type a few changes: G7, Cmaj7, E7#9, F#m7b5/C, C6/9.
3. Type something wrong (Cxyz): it says it can't read it, and the chart shows it red.
4. **N.C.** on one beat, **SAME AS BEFORE** on another, **CLEAR** on a third.
5. Put a chord on beat 3 of a bar that holds a whole note, then view ▾ › Notation: the whole note is now two tied halves, with the chord over the second.
6. All the chords show over the notation, and edits there (ADD ▾ › Chord symbols) show in the chart.

## 8. The piano (0.5.7)
With headphones, then the speaker.
1. In a score, tap piano keys from the bottom to the top of the range (scroll the octave): every note is a piano, even in level from key to key, no jump in tone where one recording hands over to the next (every minor third: B, C, E♭, F♯/G♭, A).
2. Each tapped note stops cleanly under the damper after most of a second; notes from F6 up ring on.
3. Write four quarters C D E F with **p** on the first and a crescendo to **f** on the fourth; ▶ PLAY: it gets louder note by note.
4. Add staccato to one note, an accent to another, **sfz** on a third: shorter, louder, one hit.
5. Add an acciaccatura before a note, then an appoggiatura: the first is a quick crush on the beat, the second takes half the note.
6. A trill, a mordent and a turn in F major: the upper note of a trill on A is B♭.
7. A three-note chord with an arpeggio (up, then down), a tremolo, and a pedal line under three notes: the notes under the pedal ring together until it lifts.
8. view ▾ › Chord chart, ▶ PLAY CHORDS: bass and chord at each change, again at each barline; N.C. is silent. The notation view's ▶ PLAY still plays the notes.
9. A chord-chart scrap: ▶ PLAY CHORDS at its BPM (shows NOT SET at 100); change the BPM from the button and play again.
10. In a transcription's correction screen, play the notes: they're on the piano now, louder where the note was played louder.

## 9. Velocity lane (0.5.8)
1. A score with **p** on bar 1 and **f** on bar 2: view ▾ › Piano roll. Under the roll, VEL shows hollow stems, low in bar 1 and higher in bar 2.
2. Slide one stem up: it follows the finger, shows "Velocity N", and its head fills. ▶ PLAY (notation view): that note is louder than its neighbours.
3. A chord: touching one stem moves all of its stems. Select two notes (TOOL ▾ › Select), slide one of their stems: both move.
4. Move that note in the roll, chop it, glue it back: the velocity stays.
5. EDIT ▾ › Velocity from the dynamics: the heads go hollow and the note plays at the dynamics again.
6. Scroll the roll sideways: the lane scrolls with it.

## 10. Chord symbols (0.5.9)
1. In a score in F major, chord chart view: type A#7 on a beat. The field says "Reads as A♯7, shown as B♭7 in the key"; the chart and the notation show B♭7.
2. Type Db7 in a C major score: it stays D♭7.
3. Type C2: "Reads as Cadd9" and, in copper, "Or Csus2: …". READ AS ▾ › Jazz / iReal Pro: now it reads as Csus2 with Cadd9 as the other. Try C°, C7+, Cmaj, C11 and a lowercase c the same way.
4. Type real-world spellings: C^7, C-7, Ch7, C7(b9,#11), C7-9, C-9, C6/9, C9sus, C7b9sus, Comit3, D|C, NC. Each reads (the field says as what).
5. READ AS ▾ › German: type H7, Fis-7, Es, Esus4, As7. Then READ AS ▾ › Do Re Mi: Sim, Do#7, Sol7, Lam7. ▶ PLAY CHORDS plays them.
6. Type Cxyz: it says why it can't read it, and the chart shows it red. In a chord-chart scrap, the same chord shows red there too.
7. Set READ AS back to Pop and English.

## 11. Playing along on the Board (0.5.10)
1. A project with a recording, a lyric synced to one of its takes, a tab, and a chord chart, spread down the Board so some are off screen.
2. ▶ PLAY ▾: the menu lists the recording, the tab (ON PIANO) and the chart (CHORDS ON PIANO), top to bottom.
3. Pick the tab: the Board scrolls to it; its window shows ▶ BAR 1, then 2… with the tab moving on. ■ STOP pauses it; ▶ PLAY ▾ › Resume carries on.
4. Pick the chord chart: the Board scrolls to it and the window shows the bar it's on.
5. Pick the recording: the synced lyric window shows from the line being sung, ▶ on it.
6. Open a long score, ▶ PLAY: the notation scrolls so the system being played stays at the top. Same in the tab view.

## 12. Stand mode (0.5.11)
1. On a Board with a lyric (synced to a take), a chord chart and a tab: ⋯ › Stand mode. The phone turns landscape and shows everything as one big sheet; the screen doesn't dim.
2. ▶ › the tab: the four-bar line being played is lit and stays a third of the way down as it moves on. A+ / A− change the type size.
3. ⟳: the line loops; ⟳✓ stops the loop. ⟲ goes back to the start.
4. ▶ › the recording: the synced lyric's sung line is lit and followed.
5. ● while it plays: you record into the project and the lyric stays on screen; ■ stops and the save sheet comes up.
6. ▶ › The stack (if the project has one) plays the stack.
7. ✕ or Back: portrait again, back on the Board. Turn the phone while in stand mode: it stays in stand mode.

## 13. Chord display styles (0.5.12)
1. A score in C major with chords Dm7, G7/B, Cmaj7, Bm7b5, C7(b9,#11), Bb and D|C. Chord chart view › CHORDS ▾ › Pop: Dm7, G7/B, Cmaj7, Bm7♭5, C7(♭9,♯11) with the 7 raised.
2. CHORDS ▾ › Jazz: D−7, CΔ7, Bø7; on the notation C7's ♭9 and ♯11 are stacked in a column.
3. CHORDS ▾ › Classical / analysis: ii7, V⁶₅, IM7, viiø7, ♭VII; D|C as II over I with a line between.
4. CHORDS ▾ › Plain text: ASCII.
5. Transpose the score up a minor third (ADD ▾ / EDIT transpose): F7, E♭7(♭9,♯11)… spelled for E♭ major; C6/9 becomes E♭6/9.
6. Set CHORDS ▾ back to Pop.
