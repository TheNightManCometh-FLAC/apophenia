# M4 walkthrough: Analysis and ML (S22 Ultra, Android 16)

Builds: 0.4.0–0.4.5. Nothing here has run on the phone yet. Use the plan §14.3 test recordings where you have them, and write down what was really played: that's the answer key.

## 1. Take analysis (0.4.0)
1. Record a bass riff without the click (about 92 BPM, 15 s or more). Close the save sheet and open the recording.
2. Scroll to **ANALYSIS · TAKE A**. It says "Analysing…" for a moment, then shows:
   - **TEMPO:** chips for the pulse, its half and double, and FREE TIME, plus the regularity.
   - **KEY:** up to 4 candidates, each with the notes it heard and its home-note clues.
   - **NOTES:** the notes it followed, spelled in the key.
3. The recording's BPM and KEY at the top fill in by themselves, marked ANALYSIS, unless they were already set.
4. Tap a different tempo chip or key: it becomes yours (YOURS), and running ANALYSE AGAIN doesn't change it.
5. Record the same riff **with the click**. TEMPO says the click's tempo is the take's, and the BPM stays CLICK.
6. Record something with no pulse (free-time humming): TEMPO says no steady pulse.
7. Play a pentatonic riff (A C D E G): KEY says uncertain and lists A minor and A Dorian with the same notes.
8. Strum C–G–Am–F: NOTES follows one line only, so it shows few or stray notes (chords come with Basic Pitch). KEY should still land on C major or say uncertain; tell me which.
9. Import a song: tempo and key come from the first 5 minutes; no notes beyond the first 2.

Tell me where it's wrong, with what you actually played.

## 2. Notes → MIDI with Basic Pitch (0.4.1)
1. Open a recording (a bass riff first, then a strummed chord progression). Tap **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI**.
2. The MIDI tab shows "Transcribing on the phone… N%". Time it: write down how long a 30 s take takes (the target is 1 minute of audio in under 10 s).
3. Leave the screen and open the **AI** mode: the job is under JOBS with its percentage, and then DONE. Tap it to go back to the recording.
4. The MIDI tab shows the piano roll and the notes spelled in the key. Tap the roll: the take plays from there, with a playhead.
5. **SAVE AS MIDI ▸**: pick a folder and save. Open it in FL Studio: the notes should sit where you played them (at the recording's BPM, or 120 when it has none).
6. Check it against what you played: wrong notes, octave errors on low bass, missing chord tones. Tell me which take and what was really played.
7. If it fails, the MIDI tab and JOBS say why. Send me that text (and the crash screen, if the app closes).

## 3. Finding the chords (0.4.2)
1. Strum C–G–Am–F with the click on (4/4, about 90), two bars each. Open the recording › INFO › **CHORDS**: the chart sits on the click's beats from its first downbeat.
2. Record the same strum without the click: the chart uses the analysed pulse, and says the bar line is a guess.
3. Play a chord over its third in the bass (C with E low): it should read C/E. Play power chords: E5.
4. Free-time strumming: one bar per chord, in order.
5. **MAKE A CHORD CHART ▸** opens a new chord chart with those bars, the recording's BPM and key, in the recording's project (or the Library). Edit it like any chart.
6. Older takes are analysed again once in the background, so their chords appear too.
7. Tell me which chords it got wrong, with what you played.

## 4. Correcting a transcription (0.4.3)
1. On a transcribed take's MIDI tab, tap **CORRECT ›**. The waveform is on top, the notes under it; drag sideways to move along, − and + to zoom.
2. Tap a wrong note: fix it with ▼ ▲ or OCT ▼ ▲ (the low-bass octave errors), ◀ ▶ and SHORTER/LONGER. HEAR plays the take from that note.
3. Long-press an empty place to add a missed note; DELETE a ghost note. UNDO and REDO step through it.
4. **PLAY ▾**: the take, the transcription on its own, then both together; the playhead follows.
5. **QUANTISE ▾ › 1/16** (the recording needs a BPM): the notes snap; the played ones stay outlined underneath. Off brings them back exactly.
6. Back out: the MIDI tab says "corrected" (and "quantised"); SAVE AS MIDI gives the corrected notes, quantised if it's on.

## 5. Separating stems (0.4.4)
Use one full song (plan §14.3: "one full song for stems"), imported, about 3 minutes. Headphones first, then the speaker.
1. Open the song, **AI ▾ › Separate stems** (or the **STEMS** tab › **▶ SEPARATE TAKE A**). The tab shows "Separating on the phone…" and the part count; a notification says it's working.
2. Leave the app (home button, screen off) while it runs. Come back: it should have carried on. Note how long it took from tap to done (plan §14.2 target: under 5 minutes for 3 minutes of song).
3. The STEMS tab lists VOCALS, DRUMS, BASS, OTHER. **▶ PLAY ALL STEMS** should sound like the song itself.
4. **M · S ▾** on BASS › Solo: the button becomes "▶ PLAY BASS". Play it: listen for how much drum and guitar bleeds in. Try Mute on VOCALS with nothing soloed for a karaoke mix.
5. **M · S ▾ › Open it** on one stem: it's its own recording ("<song> A · bass", tagged "stem"), and its INFO tab says **FROM** the bass stem of the song, take A ›, which goes back.
6. The four stems are also in the Library. On the bass stem, **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI** is the plan's route to learning a bass line.
7. The AI mode lists the job under JOBS while it runs and as DONE after.
8. Also try it on one of your own mono takes (a guitar or vocal idea): the stems come out the same on both sides.

## 6. Speech → text (0.4.5)
Use two or three of your own voice memos (plan §14.3): one in a quiet room, one spoken over an instrument. Write down what you actually said first.
1. Open a memo, **AI ▾ › Speech → text** (or the **TEXT** tab › **▶ WRITE DOWN TAKE A**). Time it: it should finish faster than the memo plays.
2. The TEXT tab lists lines with their start times. Tap a time: the memo plays from that line.
3. Count the wrong words against what you said, per memo (that's the plan's word error rate).
4. **EDIT**, fix a word, **SAVE**: the hint says "Corrected". **WRITE DOWN AGAIN** now warns that it loses the fixes.
5. **MAKE ▾ › A lyric sheet**: a new lyric sheet opens with the lines, in the memo's project or the Library. Try **A text note** too.
6. The AI mode lists the job and then DONE.
