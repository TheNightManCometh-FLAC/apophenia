# M4 walkthrough: Analysis and ML (S22 Ultra, Android 16)

Builds: 0.4.0–0.4.5. Nothing here has run on the phone yet. Use the plan §14.3 test recordings where you have them, and write down what was really played: that's the answer key.

## 1. Take analysis (0.4.0)
1. Record a bass riff without the click (about 92 BPM, 15 s or more). Close the save sheet and open the recording.
2. Scroll to **ANALYSIS · TAKE A**. It says "Analysing…" for a moment, then shows:
   - **TEMPO:** chips for the pulse, its half and double, and FREE TIME, plus the regularity.
   - **KEY:** up to 4 candidates, each with the notes it heard and its home-note clues.
   - **NOTES:** the notes it followed, spelled in the key.
3. The recording's BPM and KEY at the top fill in by themselves, marked ANALYSIS, unless they were already set.
4. Tap a different tempo chip or key: it becomes yours (YOURS), and running ANALYSE AGAIN doesn't change it.
5. Record the same riff **with the click**. TEMPO says the click's tempo is the take's, and the BPM stays CLICK.
6. Record something with no pulse (free-time humming): TEMPO says no steady pulse.
7. Play a pentatonic riff (A C D E G): KEY says uncertain and lists A minor and A Dorian with the same notes.
8. Strum C–G–Am–F: NOTES follows one line only, so it shows few or stray notes (chords come with Basic Pitch). KEY should still land on C major or say uncertain; tell me which.
9. Import a song: tempo and key come from the first 5 minutes; no notes beyond the first 2.

Tell me where it's wrong, with what you actually played.

## 2. Notes → MIDI with Basic Pitch (0.4.1)
1. Open a recording (a bass riff first, then a strummed chord progression). Tap **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI**.
2. The MIDI tab shows "Transcribing on the phone… N%". Time it: write down how long a 30 s take takes (the target is 1 minute of audio in under 10 s).
3. Leave the screen and open the **AI** mode: the job is under JOBS with its percentage, and then DONE. Tap it to go back to the recording.
4. The MIDI tab shows the piano roll and the notes spelled in the key. Tap the roll: the take plays from there, with a playhead.
5. **SAVE AS MIDI ▸**: pick a folder and save. Open it in FL Studio: the notes should sit where you played them (at the recording's BPM, or 120 when it has none).
6. Check it against what you played: wrong notes, octave errors on low bass, missing chord tones. Tell me which take and what was really played.
7. If it fails, the MIDI tab and JOBS say why. Send me that text (and the crash screen, if the app closes).

## 3. Finding the chords (0.4.2)
1. Strum C–G–Am–F with the click on (4/4, about 90), two bars each. Open the recording › INFO › **CHORDS**: the chart sits on the click's beats from its first downbeat.
2. Record the same strum without the click: the chart uses the analysed pulse, and says the bar line is a guess.
3. Play a chord over its third in the bass (C with E low): it should read C/E. Play power chords: E5.
4. Free-time strumming: one bar per chord, in order.
5. **MAKE A CHORD CHART ▸** opens a new chord chart with those bars, the recording's BPM and key, in the recording's project (or the Library). Edit it like any chart.
6. Older takes are analysed again once in the background, so their chords appear too.
7. Tell me which chords it got wrong, with what you played.

## 4. Correcting a transcription (0.4.3)
1. On a transcribed take's MIDI tab, tap **CORRECT ›**. The waveform is on top, the notes under it; drag sideways to move along, − and + to zoom.
2. Tap a wrong note: fix it with ▼ ▲ or OCT ▼ ▲ (the low-bass octave errors), ◀ ▶ and SHORTER/LONGER. HEAR plays the take from that note.
3. Long-press an empty place to add a missed note; DELETE a ghost note. UNDO and REDO step through it.
4. **PLAY ▾**: the take, the transcription on its own, then both together; the playhead follows.
5. **QUANTISE ▾ › 1/16** (the recording needs a BPM): the notes snap; the played ones stay outlined underneath. Off brings them back exactly.
6. Back out: the MIDI tab says "corrected" (and "quantised"); SAVE AS MIDI gives the corrected notes, quantised if it's on.

## 5. Separating stems (0.4.4)
Use one full song (plan §14.3: "one full song for stems"), imported, about 3 minutes. Headphones first, then the speaker.
1. Open the song, **AI ▾ › Separate stems** (or the **STEMS** tab › **▶ SEPARATE TAKE A**). The tab shows "Separating on the phone…" and the part count; a notification says it's working.
2. Leave the app (home button, screen off) while it runs. Come back: it should have carried on. Note how long it took from tap to done (plan §14.2 target: under 5 minutes for 3 minutes of song).
3. The STEMS tab lists VOCALS, DRUMS, BASS, OTHER. **▶ PLAY ALL STEMS** should sound like the song itself.
4. **M · S ▾** on BASS › Solo: the button becomes "▶ PLAY BASS". Play it: listen for how much drum and guitar bleeds in. Try Mute on VOCALS with nothing soloed for a karaoke mix.
5. **M · S ▾ › Open it** on one stem: it's its own recording ("<song> A · bass", tagged "stem"), and its INFO tab says **FROM** the bass stem of the song, take A ›, which goes back.
6. The four stems are also in the Library. On the bass stem, **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI** is the plan's route to learning a bass line.
7. The AI mode lists the job under JOBS while it runs and as DONE after.
8. Also try it on one of your own mono takes (a guitar or vocal idea): the stems come out the same on both sides.

### 5a. Adding Demucs and separating with it (0.9.30)
1. On the same song's **STEMS** tab › **SEPARATE AGAIN ▾**, tap **DEMUCS · ADD ›**. Two steps show. Tap **1 · DOWNLOAD IT ›**: the browser should download `htdemucs-dml.onnx` (372 MB) straight away. Note anything the browser asks.
2. When the download finishes, back in Apophenia tap **2 · PICK THE DOWNLOAD ›** and pick it from Downloads. "Adding it… %" counts up, then "Demucs is added." ✓ DEMUCS is chosen.
3. Try a wrong file once, any WAV: it should say "That isn't the Demucs file" and add nothing.
4. **▶ SEPARATE TAKE A** with Demucs. Time it from tap to done, and leave the app while it runs. If the app is closed or the phone gets slow, note it (it uses about 1 GB while it runs).
5. The stems now say **DEMUCS** under each name. Listen to bass and vocals against the Spleeter ones from step 3–4 above: bleed and the volume jumps.
6. Settings › **AI MODELS** shows "Demucs stems model · ADDED · 372 MB ▾" › **Remove it** frees the space; the steps come back.

## 6. Speech → text (0.4.5)
Use two or three of your own voice memos (plan §14.3): one in a quiet room, one spoken over an instrument. Write down what you actually said first.
1. Open a memo, **AI ▾ › Speech → text** (or the **TEXT** tab › **▶ WRITE DOWN TAKE A**). Time it: it should finish faster than the memo plays.
2. The TEXT tab lists lines with their start times. Tap a time: the memo plays from that line.
3. Count the wrong words against what you said, per memo (that's the plan's word error rate).
4. **EDIT**, fix a word, **SAVE**: the hint says "Corrected". **WRITE DOWN AGAIN** now warns that it loses the fixes.
5. **MAKE ▾ › A lyric sheet**: a new lyric sheet opens with the lines, in the memo's project or the Library. Try **A text note** too.
6. The AI mode lists the job and then DONE.

## 7. Rhythmic guitar and chord evidence (0.9.22)
Not yet device-verified. Use a 15–30 s acoustic-guitar progression without the click,
with known major/minor, power and seventh chords. Headphones first, then speaker.
1. After updating, let the recording's INFO analysis finish. Existing recordings
   reanalyse once automatically. TEMPO should give a BPM for steady strumming;
   it should not call it speech. If missing, note the displayed reason.
2. Open **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI**. Transcribe the take. For an older, uncorrected
   transcription, **TRANSCRIBE AGAIN** uses 0.9.21's revised thresholds. It replaces
   note corrections too, so retain any corrections you want to keep.
3. Return to INFO after analysis finishes. CHORDS now uses those detected notes
   alongside the sound. Compare the labels and change positions with what you
   played; a missing third must not turn into an inserted MIDI note.
4. Check the MIDI notes before/after **ANALYSE AGAIN**: analysis should change
   only its labels, never the note list. Export MIDI if useful for comparison.
5. A plain spoken memo should still show no tempo/key/chords. Short or uneven
   guitar and rhythmic speech need particular attention: the heuristic is limited.
6. A chord chart already saved as a scrap stays as written; use **MAKE A CHORD
   CHART** again to save the new analysis. No backup/interruption retest requested.

## 8. Quiet thirds/sevenths and inversions (0.9.23)
Not yet device- or ear-verified. Use an acoustic take already processed by Notes → MIDI.
1. Update and wait for INFO analysis to finish. Existing notes are reused; there
   is no need to replace your transcription or its corrections with TRANSCRIBE AGAIN.
2. Compare a quieter third/seventh in a strum with INFO › CHORDS. The chart should
   retain evidence from that detected note. Check C/E, E5, Am and G7 too, with
   headphones and then speaker. It still may miss or misname real chords.
3. Check the MIDI notes against the recording: this update changes chart evidence,
   not the note list. A missing note must remain missing until you correct it.
4. A new saved chart uses the new labels; previously saved chart scraps retain
   their written content. Basic Pitch's missing-note rate is not improved here.

## 9. Repeated notes and quiet sevenths (0.9.24)
Not yet device- or ear-verified. Use an existing transcribed guitar take with a
quieter third or seventh, including maj7/m7 voicings with octave doublings.
1. Update and wait for INFO analysis to finish; TRANSCRIBE AGAIN is not needed.
2. Compare the CHORDS labels with what you played, particularly quiet seventh
   tones. Also check C/E, power chords and the progression's root changes.
3. Check MIDI before/after ANALYSE AGAIN: its notes and timing must stay the same.
   This changes only their weighting as evidence for chord labels.
4. New chart scraps use the revised labels; saved chart scraps keep what is written.

## 10. Harmonics and chord quality (0.9.25)
Not yet device- or ear-verified. Use your acoustic-guitar take and a known piano
progression, with major/minor, seventh and power chords.
1. Update and wait for INFO analysis to finish. Existing takes reanalyse; saved
   transcriptions and corrections are reused. TRANSCRIBE AGAIN is not needed.
2. Compare INFO › CHORDS with what you played, first on a take without Notes → MIDI,
   then on one already transcribed. Listen for wrong roots, missing sevenths and
   overtones being mistaken for extra chord tones. A ringing final C should remain C.
3. Check MIDI before/after ANALYSE AGAIN: notes and timing must stay the same.
   The harmonic fit supplies chord evidence only; it must never insert a note.
4. Check tempo and chord-change positions on steady strumming. The timing trials
   were rejected; charts still have one slot per beat and may miss between-beat changes.
5. Previously saved charts retain their written content; MAKE A CHORD CHART saves
   the new analysis. The guitar-specific model is a desktop trial and is not in this build.

## 11. Choose Guitar or Basic Pitch (0.9.26)
Not yet device- or ear-verified. Use a known acoustic-guitar take, a vocal take and
an existing corrected transcription. Listen with headphones and then speaker.
1. Open a recording's **AI ▾ › Notes → MIDI**. It opens the MIDI tab before running.
   With no transcription, **MODEL** defaults to **Basic Pitch**. Open it and choose
   **Guitar** for the acoustic take, then **▶ TRANSCRIBE TAKE A**.
2. The active job and saved result must identify Guitar (GAPS). Note elapsed time
   and phone heat on the SM-S908U; model speed/RAM have not been measured there.
   Compare missing/wrong notes, strum attacks, note endings and MIDI playback with
   what you played. Try bends/slides: this model has no continuous pitch bends.
3. Return to INFO after reanalysis and compare the derived chord chart. This model
   may improve detected notes, but cannot justify inserting a note just to fit a chord.
   Use MAKE TAB · SHEET and compare its timing with the original take.
4. On the vocal take, choose **Basic Pitch**. Confirm the saved result says Basic
   Pitch; MIDI export, correction and sheet music continue to work.
5. On the corrected take, change MODEL without pressing TRANSCRIBE AGAIN. Notes,
   corrections and the saved result's model label must stay as they were. The rerun
   control says **LOSES FIXES**; use it only if you intend to replace those notes.
6. Leave/reopen a completed Guitar result: it should select Guitar from its saved
   model. If a job fails, TRY AGAIN should retain the model choice; its error must
   be visible rather than silently switching to Basic Pitch.
7. In Performance check, a take requiring transcription offers the same MODEL
   choice. Previously saved chart scraps stay as written; audio stays immutable.
